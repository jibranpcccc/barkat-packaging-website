Mango Export Packaging Standard in Pakistan

What S.R.O. 192(I)/2013 actually names as the standard mango export box weights, where that conflicts with TDAP's own published guidance, what ISPM-15 requires of the pallet underneath, and a checklist to run before the consignment leaves the packhouse.

Published 10 November 2026 · Written by the packaging team at Barkat Packaging, Plot F 9, S.I.T.E. Industrial Area, Hyderabad, Sindh. Every figure below carries its source in the reference list. Where the sources disagree, we say so instead of picking one.

1. The weights the notification names

The Ministry of Commerce issued S.R.O. 192(I)/2013 on 8 March 2013. Paragraph 4(1) and Schedule II, serial number 5, deal with the export of mangoes in standardised packaging. The notification defines the standard packaging weight of mangoes as one of fourteen values, with a variation permitted on either side of the weight stated on the box.

Fourteen steps, half a kilogram apart. The heaviest weight named anywhere in the notification is 8 kg.

This is the part most packaging pages skip. The standard is not "5 kg and 10 kg cartons". It is a continuous ladder from 1.5 kg to 8 kg, and the point of the ladder is that the declared weight on the box matches the fruit inside it. The reason the notification bothers to name fourteen steps is freight: an exporter who under-declares package weight pays less air freight than the consignment actually costs to fly, which is precisely the complaint the exporters' association made five years later.

2. Where the official documents disagree

Two government-linked sources, five years apart, do not say the same thing. This matters if you are choosing a box size, so here is both, unedited.

Ministry of Commerce, 2013

Standard packaging weights run from 1.5 kg to 8 kg. No weight above 8 kg is named.

TDAP mango export brochure

"Most frequently used box sizes are 5kg, 8kg, 10kg, and 12kg." Two of those four exceed the 2013 ceiling.

The most likely explanation is that the notification was never enforced. In May 2018 the All Pakistan Fruit and Vegetables Exporters, Importers and Merchants Association wrote to the federal Minister of Finance asking him to ensure implementation of S.R.O. 192(I)/2013, stating that customs had never given due importance to the rules. The association's letter described the consequence directly: the weight stated on the box does not conform to the actual weight of mangoes inside it, and exporters who follow the rule pay more air freight than those who do not.

What we tell our own customers

We do not know the current enforcement position, and we are not going to guess at it. If you are packing above 8 kg, or if your buyer's specification requires it, confirm with your customs agent which version of the rule is being applied to your consignment before you commit a print run and a die. A carton is cheap to change before it is printed and expensive to change after.

3. Board grade and holding temperature

TDAP's mango export brochure gives two figures that are useful because they come from the trade authority rather than from a supplier.

Parameter Published guidance Source
Board construction 3-ply or 5-ply cardboard boxes are used in the export of mangoes TDAP Mango Brochure, Packaging & Storage

Holding temperature 13 °C to 16 °C until the destination market shelf TDAP Mango Brochure

The codes of practice also name a failure mode that is worth reading twice, because it is a carton problem and not a fruit problem: weak cartons collapse, leading to poor alignment in pallets. That sentence describes a stack losing its geometry, not fruit losing its quality. Once a pallet leans, every layer above it is being loaded unevenly, and the damage shows up at the bottom of the pallet, not the top.

4. ISPM-15: the pallet under the cartons

A corrugated mango carton is manufactured board and falls outside the scope of ISPM-15. The wooden pallet, crate or dunnage it travels on does not. This is the requirement most often missed by packhouses that buy good cartons and then load them onto unmarked local skids.

Approved treatments
  • HT — heat treatment, minimum 56 °C for 30 continuous minutes throughout the full profile of the wood, including the core
  • DH — dielectric heating, 60 °C for one continuous minute, for wood not exceeding 20 cm across its smallest dimension
  • MB — methyl bromide fumigation under the conditions set out in Annex 1 of the standard
The mark must carry
  • the IPPC symbol
  • the ISO two-letter country code — PK for Pakistan
  • the code of the facility authorised by the national plant protection organisation
  • the treatment code, HT or MB

The mark is applied to the completed unit, legible and not transferable, preferably on two opposite vertical faces. For pallets the standard specifically notes the inner faces of the vertical blocks as a good location, because an inspector looking into a container can see them. The mark certifies the entire unit: it is not a statement about the cartons resting on it, and it does not travel with the cargo if the pallet is swapped at a transshipment point.

Pakistan's plant quarantine authority is the Department of Plant Protection, and Pakistan is a contracting party to the International Plant Protection Convention. The standard itself does not oblige a national authority to enforce compliance on exports, which is why the requirement that actually bites is the one from the importing country. Gulf, European and North American buyers routinely specify treated, marked wood in the purchase order whether or not the origin side requires it.

5. Working from box size to pallet plan

The single most expensive packaging mistake in a mango season is committing to a die before checking that the filled carton builds a stable pallet. The arithmetic takes about a minute and it is worth doing before the print run.

We deliberately stop at boxes per layer and do not publish a total per pallet or per container. Stack height depends on board grade, compression strength, pallet deck condition and destination handling, and a total that looks authoritative in a table is the number most likely to be wrong for your consignment. Work the layer count out, then confirm height against your board grade and your freight forwarder.

6. Pre-dispatch checklist

Every line below traces to something in the sources at the end of this page, or to a defect we have seen in our own plant.

  1. 1
    Declared weight matches filled weight

    Weigh a sample of filled cartons, not empty ones. S.R.O. 192(I)/2013 permits a variation on either side of the stated weight, but the statement has to be defensible.

  2. 2
    Board grade chosen against stack height, not habit

    3-ply or 5-ply per TDAP guidance, then sized to the number of layers you will actually build and the humidity you will actually cross.

  3. 3
    Ventilation is adequate to the pack pattern

    Slots that line up with a divider or a foam net are closed slots. Check the assembled carton, not the flat blank.

  4. 4
    Label carries traceability and clear specification

    Recommended in the codes of practice, with food-grade glue. Variety, count or grade, net weight, origin, packhouse identity and batch reference.

  5. 5
    Wood under the cartons is treated and marked

    IPPC symbol, PK, facility code, HT or MB. Ask the pallet supplier for the facility's authorisation, not a verbal assurance.

  6. 6
    Reefer pre-cooled before loading, unit off during loading

    Both recommended in the codes of practice. Loading into a warm box costs you shelf life you cannot recover in transit.

  7. 7
    Pallet built square, and it looks square

    Collapsed or misaligned cartons produce leaning pallets. Check the pallet from the corner before it goes in the container, not after.

Direct answers

The questions exporters and buyers actually ask, answered from the documents listed at the foot of this page.

What are the official mango export box weights in Pakistan?

S.R.O. 192(I)/2013, issued by the Ministry of Commerce on 8 March 2013, names standard mango export packaging in 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5, 5, 5.5, 6, 6.5, 7, 7.5 and 8 kg, with a variation permitted on either side of the stated weight. That is fourteen steps of half a kilogram. The maximum weight named in the notification is 8 kg.

Is 10 kg or 12 kg mango packaging allowed for export from Pakistan?

It depends which document you read, and this is the honest answer. The TDAP mango brochure lists 5 kg, 8 kg, 10 kg and 12 kg as the most frequently used export box sizes, and 12 kg sits above the 8 kg maximum named in S.R.O. 192(I)/2013. In May 2018 the All Pakistan Fruit and Vegetables Exporters, Importers and Merchants Association wrote to the Ministry of Finance stating that the 2013 notification had never been enforced by customs in letter and spirit. Confirm the position that applies to your consignment with your customs agent before you commit board to a size above 8 kg.

What corrugated board is used for mango export boxes in Pakistan?

The TDAP mango export brochure states that cardboard boxes of 3-ply or 5-ply are used in the export of mangoes. Board grade, burst strength and compression have to be chosen against your stack height, transit length and destination, and no single grade is right for every route.

What temperature should mangoes be held at for export?

The TDAP mango brochure states mangoes must be stored at 13 °C to 16 °C until they reach the destination market shelf. A separate Codes of Practice for Mango Farming and Processing, produced with TDAP, the Pakistan Horticulture Development and Export Company, the All Pakistan Fruit and Vegetables Exporters Association and the Government of the Punjab, recommends cleaning, sanitising and pre-cooling sea containers to the desired shipping temperature, and names 12 °C as recommended.

Does a mango carton need an ISPM-15 mark?

The corrugated carton itself does not, because processed wood packaging such as plywood and manufactured board falls outside the scope of ISPM-15. The treated wooden pallet or crate the cartons sit on does. Under ISPM-15 the wood must be heat treated to a minimum of 56 °C for 30 continuous minutes throughout the profile including the core, or treated by an approved alternative, and marked with the IPPC symbol, the ISO country code, the producer or treatment provider code and a treatment code of HT or MB.

What should be printed on a mango export carton?

The Codes of Practice for Mango Farming and Processing recommends that traceability be ensured on the package along with clear specifications, and that labels use food-grade glue. In practice that means variety, count or size grade, net weight, origin, packhouse or exporter identity and a harvest or packing batch reference, all matching the weight you declared.
